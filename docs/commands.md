# Commands

- `hidden-lines REQUEST.json` draws a Rhino model's exact hidden-line drawing as a layered SVG and DXF that pass `lint`, in place of Make2D. `rhino/hidden_lines.py`, run in Rhino through the connector (`rhino/RHINO.md`), reads the same request and writes the visible lines and the meshes into its `out` folder; this command reads them and writes `ID.svg`, `ID.dxf` and `ID-report.json` there. `hiddenlines/hidden.py`'s docstring has the method; `tests/fixtures/hidden-lines/` holds nineteen recorded scenes, each with its request: eight parallel views, seven cuts (one in perspective), two perspectives, and two of the fuzz seeds recorded as version 4 exports (`fuzz-119`, a perspective, and `fuzz-146`, a perspective cut). `--rhino OUT.json` also writes the drawing for `rhino/hidden_lines.py` to add to the model: layers and sublayers with print widths, inks and dashes, an `objects` array in scene-index order (each object's `ids` and full `layer` path), and a `source` index on every line. Paths crossing source objects are split only in this payload; `split_paths` counts the original paths split. Each sublayer's `kind` is the standard's class word, used by the optional source-layer tree in Rhino. SVG, DXF and AI keep their original paths. `--explain REDLINE.md` answers a Redline markup of the drawing: for each note it prints, under the note's marks widened by 3 pt, what each pass took out and why (the meshes see it hidden, inside a cut, a double that gave way, a thin face, a hair, a stub, a run tucked between lines, a stray, or a cut edge in no closed region), with the objects' layers and ids, what the engine hid there and what is drawn, by sublayer, and writes `ID-explain.txt` and `ID-fates.svg`, the map of all of it on the page. `--check CHECKS.json` checks the drawing against checks written from a review notes (`hidden.check_notes`'s docstring: a line drawn along a path, of a weight; a region clear of lines, or holding at least so much line, `drawn`, each counting only lines of a width between `min_w` and `max_w` if given; one line or n lines across a path; the longest break along a line, `gap`; the cut regions meeting an area, `regions`, so a poché that runs on counts one, and none fails; the notch signature, `notches`, a line's last 0.3 to 0.62 pt turning onto a line it ran beside; the join signature, `kinks`, a line's last 0.1 to 3 pt turning 8 to 45 degrees onto a line within 6 degrees of the segment before it; line ends that meet nothing, `ends`, an outline left open; line drawn off the model, `model`, further than `tol_pt` (1.5 grid steps; 0.01 for a curve in the grid's steps) from every line of the export beside the drawing; the export's visible lines dropped with no reason, `kept` (the report's `model.unexplained_at`); a note only to look at; a check given `mode`, `kept` or `simplified`, applies only to a drawing whose doubles are so, as its report says), prints which pass and writes `ID-check.txt`, and, where the checks file holds the notes' boxes and names the folder of Redline's note images (`marks`), `ID-notes/note-N.png`: each note's mark beside the drawing at Redline's own window, with its checks' results under them. Without a request, `hidden-lines --check CHECKS.json` checks the drawing the file names (`svg`). In Rhino, the `HL` command runs the export, this command and `lint` from one dialog, on the selection or everything shown, from the viewport, a standard or named view or a section from a clipping plane, each layer drawn in the role kept on it or guessed from its name, with no request file (`rhino/RHINO.md`).

  The request, in the model's units: `id`, `title`, `scale`, `out` (a folder beside the request); `view` with `bearing` (where the camera stands, in degrees clockwise from north as seen from the target) and `elevation` (degrees above the horizon), or `direction` (the way it looks) and `up`, `target` (the model point at the frame's middle) and `true_along` (a model direction drawn at true length: the site's axes in an isometric, so the scale bar holds along them); for a perspective, `lens_mm` (a 35 mm camera's lens, its 36 mm film across the frame's width), with `camera` (the eye; without it the camera stands on the view's line where the target is drawn at `scale`) and `two_point` (the camera kept level, so verticals stay vertical, the frame shifted to keep the target at its middle); `frame` (`w_pt`, `h_pt`, the window on paper round the target); `clip` (a plane `{origin, normal}` or a list of them, `normal` pointing into the part kept, the way the cut is looked at: `[0, 0, -1]` for a plan cut); `tolerance_pt` (the engine's, 0.01 on paper); `doubles_pt` (with `simplify_doubles`, lines nearer than this side by side are drawn as one: `lint.double_pt` unless given, at most `lint.near_pt`, 0 for none); `simplify_doubles` (true: a line beside a heavier one gives way under `doubles_pt`, the two drawn as one; false, the default, keeps both but where the lesser's stroke lies inside the heavier one's; an old request's `doubles_inside` changes nothing); `visibility` (`"engine"`, the default, or `"faces"`: what is seen decided from the meshes, below); `depth_bands_m` (edges in metres, `[3, 10]`, beyond the first clip plane or without one beyond the subject's nearest drawn line, context left out (a ground plane's edge in front of an elevation does not push the subject into the far bands): a line in the first band is a weight heavier than its class, never past its group's outline, one in the second keeps its class's weight, and one past each further edge is a weight lighter, each band on its own sublayer, `building overlaps 3-10 m`) or `depth_bands_share` (`[0.25, 0.6]`: the edges at those shares of how far the subject runs beyond the plane, the 95th percentile of its lines' depth with context left out; the HL dialog's new requests carry it); `objects` (ids: only these objects are collected, as the `HL` command writes it for the objects it draws); and `groups`, in order of priority, each `id` and `layers` (patterns over full layer paths, `*` crossing `::`; an object goes to the first group naming its layer) with any of `not_layers`, `where` (user text that must match), `exclude` (`user` values, or `within` a plan polygon by the box's middle), `union` (true, or a user-text key or keys whose values the parts share: `id` for a house's walls, roof, chimney and porch, `["home", "floor"]` for a home's rooms), `cut` (plan polygons whose insides go: the ground under a new building), `hides` (false for trees seen through), `draw` (false for what hides but is not drawn: curb faces), `mesh`, `ground`, `body` (groups naming one body make one mass: a building's homes, cores and galleries), `layer` (CONTEXT), `profile_layer` (OUTLINE for the subject), `profile_weight`, `weights` (a band or pt per class), `fill` or `fills` (a colour by source layer pattern), `fill_layer` (GROUND), `hidden` (true: its edges hidden behind surfaces drawn dashed on HIDDEN, a footing in the earth), `joints` (true: its parts' outlines kept inside its body's merged cut, as lines on OUTLINE in a sublayer `<body> joints` at the cut's weight unless its `weights` set `joints`; the cut stays one closed shape, so hiding the sublayer gives the merged cut back) and `above` (true, with a clip plane: what the first plane takes away from it, seen from the plane, drawn dashed on ABOVE: a plan's roof edges and beams as seen looking up). A group's `weights` are keyed by class, singular or plural (`{"cuts": "heavy"}`).

  Every line is the engine's, noded on a 0.05 pt grid (`hidden_lines.grid_pt`) so lines lying on each other become one and crossings shared nodes. A line goes only where it would print wrong: a stretch the meshes see hidden (the engine's own errors), what lies inside a cut face, and doubles. Doubles are decided by line, a chain of drawn edges of one tier and weight, from the heaviest down (the cut first, then solid lines, then dashed; by weight along the line, the engine's lines above the meshes' own edges, the nearer, group order, length). A line beside a heavier one as drawn, within 5 degrees of parallel, gives way only where its stroke lies inside the heavier one's, so nothing that prints apart is taken out; with `simplify_doubles` it gives way where nearer than `doubles_pt` (measured between the two lines as the model gives them, before snap rounding moved them up to a grid step), since the two print as one heavier line. The report's `doubles.mode` says which (`kept` or `simplified`). A stretch converging all along, closing or opening steadily at a degree or more, stays whole beside the heavier line, meeting it or not; else, where the gap closes onto the heavier line, the lesser runs on along its own path to where they meet. Where the gap stays wider the two run side by side at an even offset: the lesser gives way where the gap is under the threshold, each run decided whole, with no cut or kept stretch under 2 pt. Nothing is joined onto the heavier line, so nothing is drawn that the model does not have : a cut ends at the line's corner where the line on from it meets the heavier one, at the line's free end where the heavier line runs beside it all the way, or at a junction of the line within it; else it is not made, and both lines stay, which `lint` reports as a double, a warning. A closed line beside a heavier one all round gives way whole. A line gives way only where its object's outline stays closed and its junctions joined: a line left ending inside the cut must meet the heavier line as drawn, inside its stroke, crossing it, or running into it along its own direction where the model's lines run on there. A tile's line (a `TEXTURE` group's) ranks under any other line of its weight, and where it lies on another object's line the edge is that line's, so an outline keeps its weight through a tile lying on it. A sample with no heavier line beside it is never cut, so a lesser line is drawn across a break in the heavier one, and a break under 1 pt in the heavier line between two cuts is bridged along it. A face between lines thinner than `doubles_pt` all but a speck (kept, one the strokes round it cover) keeps only its heaviest line (kept, a line round it goes only where it lies inside the heaviest's stroke), ranked by its chain and chosen among those at least half as long round the face as the longest; a face round which every line is one object's keeps them all, as does one where a line left ending there would not meet the heaviest. A run under 2 pt in one class or depth band between two of another along one object's chain takes theirs, so a weight does not flicker along a line. An end that stops short of or runs past other linework by less than `lint.near_pt`, as drawn, is met and listed in the report with where it is in the model: run on along its own direction only where the export's lines (hidden and duplicate ones too) lie along the run-on, noded where it lies within the grid; joined to a free end of the same object under `lint.min_len_pt` away, each facing the other within 30 degrees (`gap closed`: too short to read as a gap, and the one length drawn off the model); an end beside the lines it is near, its own line within 20 degrees of each and each to its side, not ahead (a double kept, a line converging on another), stays as the model has it (the report's `ends_beside`); else cut back along its own path until it is clear by `near_pt` (`end cut back clear`), or taken whole where it is clear nowhere to its junction (`hair taken whole`); an overshoot is cut back (a stub under `near_pt` off a junction), and a hair leaving a line and staying within reach of it is taken out; an end a line taken out frees is met in the same round. A line's own stretch until it first leaves the circle of `near_pt` round its end is not linework to meet, nor, to `lint`, a path's own. Each node is drawn where the export's lines through it meet (the least-squares point of its edges' own sources, a free end at its line's own end, held within 1.5 grid steps of the node), and so are the cut regions' corners, so curves and poché run along the model's lines, not the grid's steps. Where a node is drawn further than that off an edge's own source and the edge drawn straight to it would leave the model's lines (two lines the grid noded together that the model has 0.08 pt apart), the edge runs along its source to the source's point nearest the node first (the report's `paths_to_node` counts them). The report's `model` holds the drawing to the model: `off_pt`, the drawn length further than 1.5 grid steps from every line of the export (the gaps closed, else 0); `off_grid_pt`, further than 0.01 pt (mostly lines the model gives twice a few hundredths of a point apart, drawn between them); `dropped`, every stretch of a line Rhino drew as visible that is not drawn, by reason (behind a face, under poché, a stray, a sliver seen for under `near_pt`, or the pass that took it), `named`, each reason by the object in front or the line given way to, and `unexplained_pt`, the length with no reason, with its places. A piece under `lint.min_len_pt` free at both ends goes as a stray. A line's last stretch to a free end in another class than the line it goes on from is, to `lint`, a path of its own: ending within `near_pt` of where it leaves and within 20 degrees of that line, it takes that line's class; otherwise, with its end within `near_pt` of that line, it is cut back to it, as a stub or a hair is. A drawing's lines and fills are also written as Illustrator's own document (`ID.ai`): a layer per layer, nested class and distance sublayers, each leaf at its weight and dash, each sublayer's strokes in a global swatch named for it. Each edge takes a class from the surfaces either side of it (profile, base, overlap, step, seam, crease, line) and a weight stepped down from its group's profile weight by `hidden_lines.steps` (STANDARDS.md, Line weights); each group's classes are sublayers of its kit layer (`building profiles`, or `base` rather than `base bases`). Distance leaves nest under each class as `near (under 2.05 m)`, `middle (2.05–4.92 m)` and `far (past 4.92 m)`, with numbered middles when there are more bands; the ranges use the model's units at at most three significant figures. Without bands the class is the leaf. Each leaf carries `data-weight`, which `lint` checks, the DXF entities' lineweight and the AI sublayer's swatch. SVG ids and banded AI swatch names omit the range (`BEYOND-building-profiles-near`). DXF leaves use the legal flat path `BEYOND › building profiles › near (under 2.05 m)`; the Rhino import accepts ` › ` or `::` as path separators. SVG and AI keep the original paint order by reopening a class container where other classes intervene; such split containers have the same label, and SVG gives each fragment a distinct id. Fills are the faces between the visible lines, each the colour of the surface seen in it. The report lists the model's faults the script found, each on its object with its id (`rhino/MODELLING.md`: invalid or open solids, faces crossing their own boundary, nearly planar or creased inside, edges under ten tolerances, duplicates, faces lying on each other, solids running into each other, surfaces sharing an edge unjoined, unions that failed), and this command prints them by kind; a creased face is split and a solid turned inside out is turned back on the copy drawn, and reported. Urban Studio's P2 crit isometric at 1:300 (1,007 objects in the frame, 554 parts joined into 103 solids, 1,379 contact curves, 4,714 segments) took 26 s in Rhino and 2 s here, and lints with no errors (docs/checked.md,).

  `"show_hidden": true` (false when omitted) adds the stretches of model lines hidden behind a face, dashed on HIDDEN, without changing the visible drawing. It leaves out what was clipped away, what lies inside cut poché, and anything removed as a stray, sliver or double. Hidden stretches running along a visible line within `doubles_pt` (by default `lint.double_pt`) are suppressed; coincident hidden lines are drawn once. Every group has one `<group> hidden` sublayer, all using HIDDEN's token weight and dash (the scale's fine weight if HIDDEN and its role have no weight), without class or depth weighting or group weight overrides. Groups marked `"hidden": true` still draw their hidden lines, including inside cuts; the toggle does not draw them twice. The report's `hidden_drawn` gives the drawn path count and length in points. Lint treats hidden endpoints as visibility stops and checks doubles separately among hidden and visible lines.

  With `"visibility": "faces"` what is seen is decided from the export's meshes rather than from the engine's verdicts (`hiddenlines/idbuf.py`; stage 1 of `docs/hidden-lines-plan.md`). The lines that may be drawn are every segment the engine found of a group that hides, visible, hidden or Duplicate (scene version 4 writes the Duplicate ones), but its cuts, and the meshes' own creases, boundaries and silhouettes, but a crease turning under 40 degrees on an object the export gives edge curves for (a fold of a curved surface's mesh, no edge of the model) and any where a line of the same object that is drawn (a stretch of the engine's the faces kept, or a cut) lies along one within `lint.near_pt` on the page and 0.15 pt in depth; a gap in a line the engine drew as visible, between seen stretches, that only the line's own object's mesh hides is drawn, the engine having had the object's own surfaces; and a Duplicate segment where a seen Visible one lies along it as near (one edge drawn twice: the model's pipe elbows); lines lying on each other then merge in the noding. The meshes of the groups that hide, but those on TEXTURE (a roof's tiles do not hide the roof's edges), are rasterized at 4 px/pt, each pixel centre holding its nearest triangle. Each line is sampled every 0.25 pt, and where two of the meshes' creases, boundaries or silhouettes cross it under 0.25 pt apart with no sample between, once more between them, that sample judged against every triangle of its cell (an object narrower than a step in front of a line hides it); a sample is seen where nothing covers it, or the nearest triangle covering it lies behind it or within the scene's bias in front of it along the view, or within 0.1 pt of it measured square to that surface, which keeps a curve on a faceted roof and an edge beside a face seen edge-on. A run of samples hidden at that tolerance but seen within 0.2 pt takes its neighbours' where the samples either side are both seen, so a line lying about the tolerance off a surface is not drawn in dashes (`faces.steadied_pt` in the report). That triangle is looked for among those the pixels round the sample hold, and among all those of its 2 pt cell where one of them would hide the sample but does not reach it (a face folded over another, a sliver under a pixel wide); of two lying level, the one turned to the camera is nearest. With clip planes a closed solid's back face, seen through its cut, stands for the cut face its clipped mesh lacks and hides what is not on a plane. Where a line turns hidden its end is found by halving to under a thousandth of a point, and a stretch under `lint.near_pt` of a line seen only in part is left out, as the engine's path leaves out a revived sliver. The revival of what the engine hid and the check of the engine against the meshes are not run; cuts, doubles, line ends, classes and weights are as above, and a group that does not hide keeps the engine's visible segments. The report's `faces` gives the lines tried, the samples, the meshes' edges found, tried and drawn, the length seen and the time. numba compiles the buffer's loops once, into `NUMBA_CACHE_DIR` (`~/.cache/kit-numba` unless set, off the drive). On six recorded exports  it drew the model's section AA in 97 s, against 145 s from the engine (recorded benchmark). The nearest triangle covering each sample is found in a compiled loop over the triangles in their order, with the arithmetic and ties of the numpy it replaced, and the exact samples run on numba's threads (`NUMBA_NUM_THREADS`). the model's perspective section drew in 112 s against 229 s and the model's section AA in 80 s against 130 s (14 threads; the model's in 133 s at one thread), at the same peak memory footprint, 9.6 and 4.5 GB. The drawing step also frees each array the size of the meshes once it has read it for the last time, and takes the vertices, triangles and points it queries 2^18 at a time (`hidden.CHUNK`), each query in the order it takes them whole; with that those peaks fell to 4.4 to 4.9 and 2.6 to 3.4 GB and the model's time to 105 to 109 s, the drawings unchanged . Up to 1.9 GB of a peak is freed large blocks that macOS's malloc keeps for reuse, so it varies from run to run: with `MallocLargeCache=0` the model's section peaked at 4.0 GB and the model's at 2.1 in every run, about 4 and 3 s slower. The nine reference drawings come out identical byte for byte under the default, `simplify_doubles` and `show_hidden`.

  A request with `clip` draws a section, a plan cut or a cutaway. The engine takes away the half each plane faces away from, and a closed solid of a group that hides is added so its cut face hides what lies behind it, which the engine does not do by default. Each closed solid's cut by a plane facing the camera comes from the model, exact and closed (`Intersection.BrepPlane`, `MeshPlane`), clipped by the other planes and, in a perspective, by the view: the engine's own cut segments can leave out an edge two objects share. An object without a ring (a surface) has its cut from the engine, and where it lies on another object's cut the engine keeps one of the two, the other Duplicate: scene version 4 writes those, which count among the cut's lines, and for an older export its mesh's edges on the plane, which the export's clipping makes, add what lies over 0.1 pt off its own cut segments (`mesh_cuts` in the report): a wall's cut along a box against it. A closed solid's clipped mesh is held to its rings the same way: where its section runs over 0.1 pt off them, more than the mesh's own error explains, the cut follows the mesh there and the disagreement is listed among the issues (kind `ring-mismatch`). The mesh lies off its surface by up to its tolerance, `MESH_PT` on paper, which Rhino keeps only roughly, and a face meeting the plane at a glancing angle carries that along the plane divided by the angle's sine; where four tolerances so carried explain every piece off the ring, measured square to the ring's edge beside it, the ring stands and nothing is listed (`mesh_cuts.ring_kept_pt`; the model's base profile, 0.48 pt off at a glancing side). Here each object's cut is a region, the faces inside an odd number of its rings, and a body's regions are merged where they touch, as the survey merges walls with the posts that touch them; each region is one closed path on CUT, holes and all, in a sublayer for its body (`cornella cuts`), with nothing drawn inside it and no fill. A gap between parts of a body's cut narrower than twice its weight is closed, its new edge joining the noded lines so the lines seen in the gap go and the lines running to it end on the cut as drawn. A body's cut takes its outline's weight: heavy for a group whose `layer` is CUT, the group's profile weight otherwise, so context cut by the plane stays as light as its outline. A group on CUT is what the drawing cuts, and its lines seen past the plane go on BEYOND, as `rhino/make2d_roles.py` sends a cut source layer's curves there. Where two bodies' cuts overlap (the model has them interpenetrate) the earlier group's is whole and the later's clipped by it, and the report lists the overlap; a body's cut is drawn at its first group's cut weight: as its `weights` set it, heavy for a group on a cut layer and for the ground (`"ground": true`), a rung under heavy for a group on a light layer (detail, furniture), and its profile's weight otherwise. A lighter group in a heavier body (its windows and doors, a roof's frame) is not massing: its cut is its own, closed, at its own cut weight, in a sublayer of its own (`windows cuts`), and the massing's outline is drawn over it where they meet. The massing, every body cut heavy, runs on as one poché where its cuts touch or come within the gap closed (walls into a foundation block of the ground's, a curb against a wall), drawn as the earliest body's and listed in the report's `cuts.merged`. A wall, floor or roof modelled as surfaces rather than a closed solid has no ring: what of its body's section is solid is read from its surfaces' section lines as the graph holds them, before any pass has taken one out, with its rings (`solid_cells`). A cell is solid where the surfaces round it face away from it, as the meshes' normals at the plane say, however thick (the model's right wall, an open polysurface 1 m thick); air where they face into it (a room, a cavity); and, where the sides are mixed or not read, solid if it is thinner than `hidden_lines.cut_surface_m` (0.6 m) all through. A closed solid's outline counts as facing into the cell beside it, as a solid's faces face out of it. Loose surfaces promise no side: of two near-parallel skins within `cut_surface_m` facing one way, the one facing the other is turned, and so is a skin with the section's outside behind it and another skin in front of it facing into the wall between; each turned skin is read the other way and listed in the report's issues (kind `turned`), where HL can point at it (the HL session's demo house, whose room had read as solid). A cell the sides leave mixed and thicker than that is not filled but reported (`cuts.unresolved`, printed with where it is). A line ending short of another by under `lint.near_pt` is run on to it first, so a slab whose end wall stops 0.1 pt short of its underside closes, and two skins whose free ends face away from each other are capped across within `cut_surface_m` (a roof's skins not joined at the eave). Solid cells are counted as `from_surfaces`. A body's open cut lying on a lighter body's ring is drawn over that ring at its own weight, and is not taken out as inside the lighter body's cut, nor does it give way to it as a double: a wall's cut stays heavy beside the furniture against it. A cut line that still closes no region (the ground, a roof skin) is drawn at the cut weight on OUTLINE (`ground open cuts`), since CUT holds closed paths, and a ring that does not close is reported where it is. The ground's open sheets (a terrain, the top and bottom faces of a slab of ground) are cut as one line, their top: on a vertical plane what lies under their section lines is earth, less the cut regions and what they enclose (a basement); a cut line of theirs with earth either side goes, and so does a line lying past the plane behind the earth, but not one on the plane or in front of it (`earth` in the report). A ground modelled as a closed solid keeps its own cut. A cut region thinner than `thin_cut.times_weight` its line's weight all through, in a parallel view cut square to it, is drawn at `thin_cut.share` of that weight (`building cuts thin`); a region with a thick part keeps its weight whole, so the poché is continuous. A plane seen from the half it keeps is logged, since its cut faces away. A section through the P2 site model at 1:200 (496 objects, 13 cuts of 12 objects into 3 regions) took 14 s in Rhino and 0.4 s here, and lints with no errors (docs/checked.md,).

  The finished drawing is checked against its own cut regions: the report's `verify.inside_cut` is the length of line drawn inside them shrunk by 0.01 pt, but the joints and the dashed hidden lines, with where its longest piece is in the model, and the summary prints it with the cuts; it is 0 unless a pass after the cuts' put a line back.

  A request with `lens_mm` draws a perspective. A camera point goes on paper at `L x / -z` and `L y / -z` on the request's scale, less the frame's shift, `L` being the depth drawn at `scale`; every depth along a line or across a triangle is interpolated as `1 / z`, which is affine on paper where depth is not. Objects wholly behind the camera, outside the view or in the half a plane takes away are left out; meshes are clipped by the planes and the camera's near plane; the curves that lie on surfaces are moved toward the camera by a scaling about it, which keeps their projection, and put back after. The engine computes a perspective in a projective space whose depth unit is half the farthest depth it draws; its tolerance and that scaling are set from an estimate of that unit, and the drawing is computed again with the engine's own where the two differ by more than 5 percent. `true_along` needs a parallel view.

- `lint FILE.svg|FILE.dxf [--scale N]` finds open cuts and near misses (errors), strays, duplicates (but a heavier cut drawn over a lighter cut's edge, which prints the same), lines running side by side nearer than `lint.double_pt` (doubles, summed over each pair of paths and reported once they run 3 pt so, five to a pair of layers and the rest counted; in a hidden-line drawing that keeps its doubles, its `<desc>` or DXF header saying so, counted in one warning), line ends in a hidden-line drawing lying beside lines that run within 20 degrees of their own, to their side (beside: counted in one warning, not a near miss), off-token weights and colours, labels over lines, and layers with no role (warnings). An SVG from Illustrator needs `--scale`; its class styles and px lengths are read. A DXF's splines, ellipses, polyline arcs and blocks are flattened, and entities it does not read are listed. A role guessed from a layer's name is reported, never checked against. A map's SVG is checked only where it prints through its window's clip. Exit 1 on an error.
